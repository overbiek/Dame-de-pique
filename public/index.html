How to play

The idea

Four players, a full deck, thirteen cards each. A game runs to 16 rounds and the highest total wins.

Unlike most versions of this game, points here are something you want. You score for winning tricks and lose points for the hearts and the queen of spades you pick up along the way.

Before the first round

Everyone cuts a card. Highest card deals first, and the deal then passes clockwise after every round.

Passing

After the deal you choose 2 cards to hand to another player. The direction changes each round and repeats in a cycle of four:

  • L to the player on your left
  • R to the player on your right
  • O over the table, to the player opposite
  • K keep — nobody passes anything

Everyone passes at the same time, so you give your cards away before seeing what you get back.

Playing a trick

The player to the dealer's left plays the first card. Going clockwise, everyone adds one card, and whoever played the highest card of the suit that was led takes the trick and leads the next one.

You must follow the suit that was led if you hold it. Only when you have none of that suit may you play whatever you like — which is your chance to get rid of something expensive.

Suits never trump one another. A card of a different suit can never win a trick, no matter how high it is.

The opening card

The very first card of a round may not be a heart or the queen of spades. From the second card onwards there are no restrictions at all — hearts can be led straight away on the next trick.

This resets every round. The only exception is if the player opening holds nothing but hearts and the queen, in which case they may lead one.

Scoring

Everything in a trick goes to whoever wins it:

Winning a trick+10
Each heart in that trick−its value
The queen of spades−26

Hearts cost what they show: 2♥ is −2, right up to J♥ −11, Q♥ −12, K♥ −13 and A♥ −14. Every other card is worth nothing.

So a clean trick is a straight +10. A trick holding the A♥ is 10 − 14 = −4. A trick with the queen of spades in it is 10 − 26 = −16.

Shooting the moon

If a single player takes all thirteen hearts and the queen of spades in one round, the whole round is thrown out and scored like this instead:

The player who did it+60
Everyone else−20

Tricks won that round no longer count for anyone. It is a real gamble: fall one heart short and you are left holding every penalty card instead.

Reading the scoresheet

After each round you get the full sheet — one row per round, one column per player, with each round's letter (L R O K) showing which way the cards went. Totals sit along the bottom, and a figure in gold marks a round where somebody shot the moon.

A quirk worth knowing: every round adds up to exactly zero across the four players. The 130 points from thirteen tricks cancel the 130 points of penalties precisely. If a round doesn't total zero, something has gone wrong.
